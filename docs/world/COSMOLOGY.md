# Nexus Verge — Cosmology

Two things make Aevorn what it is. The first is geography: it is a convergence point, where the edges of countless dimensions fold together (see `docs/world/WORLD.md`). The second is that it sits close enough to both of the forces beneath everything to feel each of them directly — **the Source** and **the Void**.

This doc is the authority on what those two are. It does not make either of them a character, a will, or a side.

---

## How to Read This Doc

Three tiers, kept strictly separate. Writing content in the wrong tier is the most common way this canon gets broken.

| Tier | What it is | Who may state it |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Author truth** | Correct. The actual shape of things. | Nobody, ever, in player-facing text. Not an NPC, not a book, not a quest resolution. |
| **B — Belief** | What cultures and scholars hold. Partial, incompatible, sincerely argued. | Any NPC, in their own culture's register. |
| **C — Discoverable** | Observable facts a player can accumulate in play. | The world itself, through items, terrain, and consequence. |

Tier A exists so that Tier B contradictions stay *coherent* contradictions rather than noise. A player who collects every Tier C observation and every Tier B theory should be able to feel the shape of Tier A without the game ever having confirmed a word of it.

## Tier A — The Source

### What it is

**Everything is everything.** It is all just the cosmos.

The Source is not a place, a being, a plane, or an energy. It is the single continuous cosmos, with no edges anywhere in it. Not many things arranged without hierarchy — *no separate things at all.* No boundary between here and there, between knower and known, between a person and everything a person is not.

Separateness is the local condition ordinary existence runs on. A rock is a distinct rock, you are a distinct you, that ridge is three days away — these are true in the way a floor is true, which is to say true enough to build on and not true underneath. The Source is what is there when separateness stops holding.

Nothing in Aevorn is powered by the Source. Nothing draws on it, spends it, or is granted anything by it. There is no reservoir of it to find, because it is not a supply — it is the condition of which every distinct thing is a local exception.

### One fact, three surprised witnesses

Everything attributed to the Source is the same fact reported by observers who assumed separateness and were each surprised in a different direction.

- **A fount of knowledge** — not an archive that gives answers out. There is no gap between knower and known to cross, because knowing and being are not two things. Anyone who touches this reports it as *knowing*, because that is the only word they have for arriving at a fact. It was never an arrival.
- **A means of transport** — not a shortcut, not a fast route. There is no distance to cross. Separation was never real, so acting through the Source is acting on what is actually the case instead of on the working illusion. This is what **Threading** does, and why nobody who does it can explain it.
- **A capacity to act** — not power granted, not a force wielded. If nothing is apart from you, nothing is outside your reach, and nothing was ever waiting to be reached.

Omniscience, omnipresence, and omnipotence do not need three explanations. They are one property, described by three witnesses who each kept the assumption that made it inexplicable.

### What the Source is not

- **Not flatness. Not overload.** This is the most important line in the section. The Source is *not* an overwhelming quantity of separate things weighted equally, none louder than another. It is not sensory saturation, not too much detail, not a signal you fail to pick out of noise. There is no noise, because there is nothing to pick apart. Attention does not fail. **Boundaries** fail.
- **Not benevolent.** It has no preference. It does not favour life, order, the Wrought, or Aevorn. If the Source were on anyone's side, the Unmooring would not be happening.
- **Not conscious.** It does not know anything, want anything, or notice anything. There is no it-and-you for a noticing to happen across.
- **Not a resource.** No item draws power from it, no calling channels it, no ritual petitions it.
- **Not divine, and not serene.** See the Writing Rules. This is the trap the whole doc exists to prevent.
- **It has no boundary, no creatures, and no envoys.** The Void has a boundary because it is a neighbouring construct. The Source is not next to anything. There is no Source equivalent of the Voidborn, no "Sourceborn," and no faction that arrives from it. Do not invent one.

## Tier A — The Void

### The hard part, stated plainly

The Void is not the removal of the Source. It is a construct in which the Source was never a term.

The Void does not take things away. On its construct, the things were never there to take. There is no "was" for it to have destroyed — absence in the Void is not a subsequent condition, it is the original and only one.

Say it against the near misses, because every one of them is a downgrade:

- **Not ignorance.** Ignorance is a mind lacking a fact that exists. The Void is the fact not existing.
- **Not death.** Death is a thing that lived ending. The Void has no ending because it has no beginning to end.
- **Not blindness.** Blindness is failing to see something present. The Void is not a failure of perception. There is nothing being failed at.
- **Not darkness, not evil, not entropy's mascot.** WORLD.md's existing "What the Void Is Not" list stands unchanged; this is the layer under it.

The closest honest approximation the setting can make: *it is not that the region is gone. It is that the region's never having been is now also true, and both remain true at once, and only one of those two answers has anywhere to go.*

### Why the boundary moves

The Void is not advancing at Aevorn. It has no capacity to advance at anything. Two constructs are in contact, and along that contact there are places where "does this exist" has two incompatible correct answers. The boundary is not a front line. It is the width of that disagreement, and it is not uniform, not straight, and not stable.

Nothing decides where it goes next. Nobody is targeted. A settlement is not chosen.

### The carried memory

Regions lost to the Void are still *remembered* — by the people who escaped them, by the maps that recorded them, by the names the Sirathi kept. This should be impossible. On the Void's construct there is nothing to have remembered.

Author truth: memory persists because a mind is still a boundary that holds. It is still apart, still a distinct thing, and a thing that is still distinct is still a record. The Wrought carry the last evidence that a place ever was, and they carry it in the only medium the Void has not reached. Whether this holds indefinitely is not established, including here.

This is why Sirathi grief is load-bearing canon and not decoration. A Sirathi is not mourning a place that was destroyed. They are the last thing keeping it true.

No culture has a term for this. Nobody has framed it this way. Do not let an NPC explain it.

### Voidborn under this frame

Voidborn are not envoys, fragments of a will, or servants of anything. They are the boundary condition made local — a volume in which the Void's terms locally apply. They propagate toward complexity because complexity is where separateness is most elaborate: the most structure, the most distinct things being distinctly themselves, and therefore the most that the Void's construct has no term for.

`docs/world/CREATURES.md` remains authoritative for how Voidborn are written and how they are named per culture. Nothing there changes. The addition is why they *are*, not how they read.

**Scale rule (hard):** retroactive absence operates at regional scale over long timescales. At the scale of a fight, the Void reads as *reduction* — things stop working as intended, vitality lowers, sound thins. A Void Spawn's touch does not unmake your childhood. This split is deliberate and protects both the shipped combat text and the cosmic register; do not collapse it in either direction.

**Voidborn inland.** Within living memory, Voidborn have stopped being a boundary-only phenomenon. They now appear in regions with no mapped boundary anywhere near them — inland, in settled country, in places with roads. The Vethri say this is the same process at a wider radius and are not surprised. Everyone else finds it new and unbearable. This is the canonical explanation for why Voidborn appear across the world map rather than only at its edges.

## Tier A — Neither Is the Good One

Both forces annihilate the individual. They do it oppositely, and that symmetry is the centre of the whole cosmology.

| | What happens to you |
|---|---|
| **The Void** | You never existed at all. Absence so complete it is retroactive. |
| **The Source** | You were never *separate*. Not erased — never bounded in the first place. |

Neither of these is a mercy and neither is a punishment. Union with everything reads as a blessing in most fiction. Here it is the loss of every boundary that lets a person be a person, and it is exactly as unsurvivable as absence. The Source is not the safe one. It is the other one.

The Source will feel benevolent to anyone living inside its consequences, because they are living inside its consequences, and the Void will feel malevolent to anyone losing a home to it. Both readings are artifacts of standing where the Wrought stand. Neither force has a position.

The Source does not object to the Unmooring. It cannot. The Void does not intend it. It cannot.

**The objection is entirely ours.** That is the setting's actual moral centre, and it is a stronger one than a cosmic villain would be. Nothing in the cosmos is on Aevorn's side except the people standing on it.

## Tier A — How This Explains What Already Exists

Nothing below is a retcon. Each of these already reads correctly at its surface; this is only the layer underneath, and the surface descriptions do not change.

| Established thing | Author-level mechanism |
|---|---|
| **The Convergence** | Aevorn is where separateness holds most loosely — where *elsewhere* is thinnest. Fragments of other dimensions do not fall in so much as fail to stay apart from here. |
| **Riftborn materials** | Matter that has crossed membranes repeatedly is less separate from what it was elsewhere. Its impossible properties are not two substances combined — they are one thing that never fully became two, which is exactly why it cannot be manufactured in a single-dimension world. |
| **Threading** | Acting on the fact that there is no distance, rather than on the working illusion that there is. Arrived at empirically over eons and understood theoretically by no one. Threaders talk about routes, thresholds, and what a thread will carry. Never about mechanism, because they don't have one. |
| **Threading fails Void-adjacent** | Threading works because there is nothing genuinely apart from the destination. The Void offers no destination to not-be-apart-from. This is why nobody simply evacuates a threatened region by rift, and it should be treated as a hard constraint on any quest design. |
| **Unheld** | What the boundary produces where both failures meet: matter that is neither reliably a distinct thing nor reliably a thing that was. Extraordinarily potent for exactly that reason. Harvesters are consumed because proximity spreads the condition, not because it burns them. |
| **Godsign relics** | Objects that are not fully separate objects. They resist appraisal because "what is this made of, and how old" assumes an edge they do not entirely have. They do not degrade because wearing down is something that happens to a thing distinct enough from its surroundings to lose material to them. They respond near the Void because that is where the two annihilations meet. Their surface descriptions stay exactly as shipped. |
| **Vethri calibration** | Centuries of living where the two constructs disagree, adapting to it without ever theorising it. Their minimalism is empirical, not philosophical — they reduced until the boundary stopped costing them, and never wrote down why. |
| **Void-adjacent behavioural drift in fauna** | Animals adjusting to a locally thinning world faster than the Wrought do, because they are not arguing with it. |

### The two working words

Both of the Verge's practical cosmological terms are folk coinages, not scholarship. Neither describes the mechanism. Both are correct anyway, which nobody involved finds remarkable.

**Threading** — dimensional travel. The word comes from the Verathi habit of calling Aevorn *the deep weaving*: a thread is a line pulled crudely through something far larger than the person pulling it. A practitioner is a **threader**; a route is **a thread**; you **thread a crossing**. Threaders are tradespeople, not theorists — the vocabulary is a loom's vocabulary, and the whole discipline is empirical technique with nothing behind it. Threads hold or they don't. A threader can tell you which, and can't tell you why.

Per-culture: Verathi say *threading* and have for eons. Kethara say *threading* and mean the ferry fare. Vaethori administrative records say *sanctioned transit* and mean threading. Delhari refuse the word on principle and say *position-state transit*, which is longer and no more accurate.

**Unheld** — what the boundary produces. In the Verge's register, to be *held* is to be held apart: kept a distinct thing with edges of your own. Unheld is what is left where that stops. A Vethri coinage, used as a mass noun with no article: *"Unheld doesn't keep."* They named it because they were the only ones standing there when it needed a name, and they named it what it is. Outsiders soften it to *the unheld*, which the Vethri do not correct.

Do not let either word carry more freight than a trade term. Nobody says "Threading" reverently, and nobody says "Unheld" as if it explains anything.

## Tier A — The Gods, Reframed

`docs/world/GODS.md` is the authority on the Departure and on godsign relics. This is the cosmological layer under it, and it changes none of that doc's surface.

The theory that gods passed through Aevorn survives intact. What this doc adds is what a god would have to *be* for the theory to work: not a species, not a rank, not a maker, and not a being the Source granted anything to. A god is something that let the illusion of separateness go further than anything else has — less apart from everything than a person is.

Under that reading the three classical attributes stop being supernatural and become tedious, for the reasons above: they are one property with three names.

Which means the Departure has three available readings, and this doc confirms none of them:

1. **They stopped being individuated.** They did not travel anywhere. There stopped being a distinct thing that could have departed. This is the reading the cosmology makes easiest, and it is still only a reading.
2. **They went the other way** — separateness reasserted, and whatever they became is still here, indistinguishable from anyone.
3. **They left the way anything leaves a convergence dimension** — by the same boundary mechanics the Void uses now. This is the existing Vethri position in GODS.md. It survives intact.

**This must not become worship.** There is nobody listening, nothing granting, and nothing to petition. GODS.md's writing rule — **no pantheon, no divine casters, no church** — is binding here and is not softened by anything in this doc. If a Delhari cult believes the dissolution of separateness is an attainable technique, that is a Tier B belief and a very good reason for them to be dangerous. It is not a mechanic, and it is not a reward.

## Tier A — What Stays Unresolved

Deliberately open, including at author level. Do not close these in a quest.

- **What was already here before the Convergence** (`docs/world/HISTORY.md` §I). The tempting answer — that "something already here" and the Source are the same thing described by a witness who assumed it was a something — is *available* and *unconfirmed*. This doc declines to settle it on purpose; it is one of the setting's load-bearing open threads.
- **Whether the Source and the Void are two constructs or one described from two positions.** The Delhari argue both. Nothing decides it.
- **Whether the boundary can be stopped, slowed, or only outlived.**
- **Whether carried memory holds indefinitely.**

## Tier B — Cultural Takes

Six partial readings. All sincere. All incomplete. None correct. Do not let any two agree more than they do here.

**Verathi** — They have a word for it and refuse to use it as a name: *Aevorel*, "that which does the weaving," a description rather than a title. Two reasons, and they will give you the first: naming a thing is a claim, and they are stewards, not owners. The second they mostly leave implied — a name marks a thing off from everything else, and they suspect that is exactly the mistake. Their position is that this is not a subject to be resolved, only a subject to keep arguing, and they have been arguing for eons. On the Void: not new, not an event, and everyone else's insistence on calling it one is evidence of how recently everyone else arrived.

**Vethri** — Have no word for the Source at all, and consider this correct. You do not name the floor. They have exactly one word for the Void and it is *it* — "It comes whether we speak of it or not." They will not elaborate, not out of secrecy, but because elaboration has never once helped. The one thing they did name is **Unheld**, because they were the only people standing at the boundary when it needed a name. They are closer to Tier A than any other culture and have the least interest in saying so.

**Delhari** — Call it "the undifferentiated continuum" or "first-order state," terminology imported from origin-worlds where it meant something adjacent, and argue continuously about whether it is one phenomenon or several — which is the one question its own premise answers, and they have not noticed. Call the Void "the entropic boundary event," which is wrong in a way that would take them a generation to see. A meaningful minority hold that dissolving separateness is a technique rather than a condition, and that it can be practised. These are the ones who get people killed.

**Kethara** — Call it *the give* — "there's give in it," the way a tool-user talks about a joint that isn't quite fixed. Things aren't nailed down as tight as they look. That is the entire theory and they consider it sufficient. On the Void: weather with worse consequences. They do not find the cosmology interesting and slightly distrust anyone who does.

**Vaethori** — Officially, not a subject of imperial administration; the Void is a natural phenomenon requiring administrative response. Stoic doctrine files both under *what is not in our capacity*, which is doctrinally correct and, in practice, a way of not looking at it. There is a deeper reason the empire cannot engage: an administration runs on things being countable, bounded, and distinct — a parcel, a citizen, a province. A cosmos with no edges in it is not a heresy to them. It is unadministrable, which is worse. Their surveys are unknowingly the largest body of Tier C evidence anyone has assembled, and nobody has been assigned to read it as one.

**Sirathi** — No cosmology at all, and no patience for anyone else's. Their word for what came before is *the before*, the same words they name their settlements with, and they use it for a lost region and for the condition of the universe interchangeably — not as metaphor, just as the only vocabulary that ever fit. They also have the sharpest instinct of anyone that being a distinct thing is not guaranteed, having watched a place stop being a distinct place. If a Sirathi has a position on the Source, it is that it clearly does not intervene, and they resolved their feelings about that some time ago.

## Tier C — What a Player Can Discover

Observable in play. Each proves something small and refuses to prove the large thing behind it.

| Observable | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Godsign relics warm near the Void boundary | The relic is coupled to something the boundary affects | What. Nobody measures it the same way twice. |
| No assayer agrees twice on a godsign relic's material or age | Some objects do not have one answer | Why an object would fail to have one |
| Riftborn materials hold contradictory properties at once | Convergence produces impossible-but-stable matter | That this is the same phenomenon as anything else |
| Threads fail near the boundary | You cannot evacuate a region by rift | Why — only that it doesn't work |
| Unheld consumes anyone who harvests it in quantity | It is not safely extractable at scale | What the condition it spreads actually is |
| Voidborn appearing in settled inland regions | The phenomenon is widening | Why, or whether it accelerates |
| A Sirathi names a place that is not on any current map | Memory outlasts the thing remembered | That the memory is doing anything |
| Six cultures give six incompatible accounts | Nobody knows | That any of them is closer |

**Hard rule:** the game never confirms the cosmology. No NPC is right. No book is authoritative. No quest ends with an explanation. Content that resolves the Source or the Void — a revelation, an artifact that "proves" it, a sage who finally knows — is out of canon regardless of how well it is written.

## Writing Rules

### Writing the Source

**Trap one: holiness.** The moment the Source reads as sacred, the setting has a religion, and GODS.md's rule is broken.

**Trap two, and the more dangerous one: serenity.** Losing every boundary that makes you a person is not peace, not arrival, and not a reward. Most fiction writes union with everything as a blessing. This setting writes it as the other way to stop existing. If a passage would make a reader want it, the passage is wrong.

**Trap three: flatness.** The Source is not overload, not everything-at-once-shouting, not a person unable to prioritise. That is a different and rejected idea. Boundaries fail; attention doesn't.

- Never: divine, holy, blessed, sacred, grace, providence, radiance, light-as-goodness, purify, cleanse, chosen, worthy, "the Source wills/wants/allows."
- Never: transcendence, enlightenment, oneness, harmony, at peace, ascension, awakening, becoming whole.
- Never assign it agency, even by accident. "The Source provides" is wrong. "There is no distance" is right.
- Do not let a character be grateful *to* it. Gratitude implies a recipient and a giver, which is the exact assumption being denied.
- It is never a power source. If a description implies an item or ability draws on the Source, rewrite it.
- **Nobody in the Verge has good words for this.** Every character who tries should sound slightly wrong in their own mouth — reaching for a borrowed word, correcting themselves, or stopping. There is no fluent speaker of this subject anywhere in Aevorn, and there should never be one.

- **Wrong:** "A blade blessed by the Source, radiant with the light of creation."
- **Right:** "The edge has never needed restoring. Nobody can say what it's made of, and nobody has asked it twice."

- **Wrong:** "For a moment he felt the peace of belonging to everything."
- **Right:** "For a moment he could not have said where his hands stopped. He did not describe it afterward. He didn't have it to describe."

### Writing the Void and absolute absence

**The trap is destruction.** Erasure is still an event with a victim. Absolute absence is not.

- Never: consumed, devoured, destroyed, erased, claimed, taken, swallowed, hungry, spreading-as-infection.
- Never give the Void or Voidborn a verb that implies a target. They do not do things to you. Conditions apply, or do not.
- Never write the loss of a region as a moment. It is not a moment; it is an answer becoming true.
- Never let a character correctly explain absolute absence. If it lands in-game, it lands as vertigo, not as exposition. The right NPC line stops one sentence short of the idea.
- Keep the scale rule: encounter-scale Void reads as *reduction*; regional-scale reads as *never-was*. Never write a Voidborn attack as retroactive unmaking.
- CREATURES.md's Voidborn writing rule (no rage, no hunger, no malice — weather and physics) is unchanged and remains the operative rule for all creature text.

- **Wrong:** "The Void devoured Old Moru, erasing it from history."
- **Right:** "There's no road to Old Moru. There's no place the road would go. Ask a Sirathi and they'll still tell you how far it was."

- **Wrong:** "The Void Spawn's touch unmakes what you were."
- **Right:** "Whatever it touches stops working as intended."

### Vocabulary

**Use (setting-native register):** apart, held apart, separate, separateness, bounded, edge, edges, distinct, continuous, no distance, thin, thinning, boundary, calibration, give, thread, threading, threader, Unheld.

**Never use (physics-lecture register):** quantum, particle, wave, superposition, observer, entanglement, probability, field, vacuum, dimension-as-jargon. The concept is quantum in nature; the *vocabulary* is not. Aevorn has cataloguers and quiet people who know things. It does not have a physics department.

**Never use (IP/SRD risk):** any proprietary cosmology term. No named outer planes, no proprietary energy taxonomies, and specifically not "the Weave" — the Verathi weaving imagery uses *Aevorn*, *Aevorel*, *weftglass*, and *threading*, and stops there.

**Retired vocabulary — do not reintroduce:** *wayfinding* / *wayfinder* (→ threading / threader) and *null-flux* (→ Unheld). These were early-draft coinages that never reached player-facing text. The unrelated `desert_wayfinding` skill challenge in `data/skillChallenges.json` is ordinary desert navigation and is not affected, though the ID reads badly next to this ruling and is worth renaming when that file is next touched.
