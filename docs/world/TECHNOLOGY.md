# Nexus Verge — Technology

## The Scavenging Model

Aevorn doesn't have a technology tree. It has an inbox. Convergence delivers fragments of countless origin-worlds at every tech level simultaneously — the same mechanism that deposits riftborn materials, biomes, and peoples deposits tools, weapons, and machines nobody on the Verge invented and few can fully explain. A hand-forged plow and a device that shouldn't have a name yet can turn up in the same ruin, from different dimensions, centuries apart in origin and minutes apart in arrival.

Almost nothing here is manufactured start to finish on Aevorn. What exists is repair, adaptation, and reuse: an object built somewhere that made sense of it, now surviving somewhere that doesn't. Most anachronistic tech can't be locally reproduced even when it can be locally operated. When it breaks, it usually stays broken.

This is why the Verge's baseline texture is medieval-fantasy-frontier despite technology existing at every level above and below that line: functioning, *reproducible* tech is rare specifically because reproduction is the hard part, not arrival.

## Writing Rule

Anachronistic technology should read as **an event, not a setting.** It intrudes rarely and startles when it does — a light source with no visible flame, a device that clearly isn't a crossbow, a machine humming in a ruin that's otherwise gone quiet for three hundred years. If a scene's baseline texture starts to feel modern rather than frontier, the anachronism has stopped intruding and started replacing it — pull back.

- **Wrong:** A market stall casually selling parts for three different eras of firearm.
- **Right:** One merchant, once, has a single component nobody can identify, and won't say where she got it.

**Exception — one normalized tier vs. casual variety:** the "event, not a setting" rule governs *variety*, not existence. A single tech tier that arrived once and has since been crudely reproduced into a recognized (if inconsistent) local weapon or tool category is the reproduction model working as intended — it's baseline-common precisely because that one thing diffused, and every copy still shows the seams (badly-relined barrels, mismatched maker's marks, "no two look quite alike"). That's different from the market-stall case, which fails because *multiple distinct eras/tiers* of anachronistic tech are sitting side by side as ordinary stock. One recognized outlier reads as frontier; a spread of them reads as a tech tree.

## Cultural Takes

**Delhari** — Most likely to arrive *with* working tech from their origin-world, and the most likely to over-explain it — the same reflex they apply to the Void. A Delhari doesn't say "that's a strange lantern." They say "output is consistent with a cold-fusion derivative, though the housing predates any fusion theory I can place it in."

**Vaethori** — Institutional, not deployed. Recovered anachronisms go to classified imperial research postings rather than the front line. The empire values order and predictability over any single advantage a half-understood device might grant — reinforcing the same administrative identity that builds roads and keeps the census. A found artifact is logged, catalogued, and quietly disappears into Vaethoran custody.

**Kethara** — Pragmatic scavengers, no ceremony. If it works, it gets jury-rigged into the wall. A Kethara engineer doesn't ask what a device was built for — they ask what it can be made to do now. "Don't know what it used to be. Now it's a door latch."

**Verathi** — Treat anachronistic tech as data about convergence itself, not a resource to exploit. They study what arrives the way they study everything else that's fallen through Aevorn — evidence toward a longer pattern only they're patient enough to be tracking.

**Sirathi** — Personal, not academic. Debris of a lost home's technology surfaces sometimes, and for the Sirathi it isn't a puzzle — it's grief with moving parts. A Sirathi who recognizes a fragment of home-world tech goes quiet in a way that has nothing to do with whether it still works.

**Vethri** — Wary of complexity on principle, and this is the practical half of that principle, not just the aesthetic half: Voidborn propagate toward order and structure, and added technological complexity may be an added attractant near the boundary. Vethri minimalism isn't only austerity — it's risk management. "Don't build what draws it closer."

## Resource Competition

Recovered technology is a third contested resource, alongside riftborn materials and null-flux (see `docs/world/WORLD.md`). Unlike raw materials, tech relics carry information as well as value — whoever holds one may hold the only working example of something nobody else can build. This sharpens the political friction described in `docs/world/CREATURES.md`'s rival-sapient competition note: a contested ruin isn't just fought over for what it's made of, but for what still runs inside it.
