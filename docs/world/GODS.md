# Nexus Verge — Gods

## The Departure

Gods passed through Aevorn, the same as everything else that converges here. That is the theory, and it will never be more than a theory — no culture has resolved it, and nothing in this doc should be read as a resolution. What the theory proposes: something with the properties people elsewhere call divine arrived during the Convergence, took no lasting form, established no worship, and at some point stopped being here. Not destroyed. Not defeated. Departed — to a destination nobody has ever confirmed, the same way nobody can confirm where a region lost to the Void actually goes.

This slots into the oldest unresolved fragment in Aevorn's history: that something was already here before the Convergence properly began (see `docs/world/HISTORY.md` §I). A minority of Verathi hold that the "something already here" and any departed gods are the same presence, described two ways by two different kinds of witness. Most Verathi consider this a stretch. Nobody has evidence to settle it either way.

## Godsign Relics

The only tangible trace, if it's a trace at all: a small number of legendary items across the Verge that behave like nothing else that's ever converged here. **Godsign** is the folk name for the pattern, not a confirmed classification.

Traits attributed to godsign relics:

- **They resist appraisal.** No assayer, archivist, or Delhari classification framework produces the same answer twice about what they're made of or how old they are.
- **They do not degrade.** Centuries or millennia of use leave the objects around them worn, but never the relics themselves.
- **They respond, faintly, near the Void.** Isolated reports of subtle change — warmth, resonance, a shift in weight — when a godsign relic sits close to Void-adjacent conditions. Nobody has proposed a mechanism. Nobody has replicated it reliably enough to study.

None of this proves anything. It's exactly the kind of ambiguous, contested evidence Aevorn produces about everything, including itself.

## Writing Rule

**No pantheon. No divine casters. No church.** This is lore and flavor exclusively — none of the three Callings are divine casters, and no faction in the Verge organizes around confirmed worship. Godsign relics are legendary items with an origin story attached, not a mechanical category. A godsign relic's stat block never changes because of this lore; only its description does.

## Cultural Takes

**Vaethori** — Officially agnostic; the empire establishes no religion, and its Stoic doctrine treats the question as outside anyone's capacity to answer, therefore not worth dwelling on. Unofficially, soldiers at forward postings privately keep small votive godsign tokens — a quiet, unspoken contradiction of the official line that nobody in the chain of command chooses to notice.

**Sirathi** — The most devotional take, for the obvious reason: people who have already watched a world end find "even the gods left" the easiest of all theories to believe. A godsign relic, to a Sirathi, functions less as evidence and more as the last tangible link to whatever was lost — proof that something enduring passed through their home too, once.

**Kethara** — Folk superstition, told the way Kethara tell everything: flatly, without ceremony. "The old gods spooked and ran, same as everyone eventually does out here." Not reverence. Recognition.

**Delhari** — An open academic question and a cataloguing project. Every Delhari scholar has a godsign theory; none of them agree, and most enjoy the argument more than they'd enjoy a resolution.

**Vethri** — Quietly track a theory that the gods left by the same boundary mechanics the Void uses now — that departure and the Void's advance might be the same kind of event, at different scales. They say almost nothing about it, as always, and only to people they've decided need to know.

**Verathi** — See "The Departure" above: a minority, controversial position holds that the departed gods and the pre-Convergence presence are identical. Most Verathi treat this as an overreach of an already-uncertain fragment, but it is not dismissed outright — nothing that old ever is.
