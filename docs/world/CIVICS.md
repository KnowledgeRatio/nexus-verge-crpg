# Nexus Verge — Civics & Governance

Six cultures, six different answers to "who's in charge, and why does anyone listen." None of these models were built with the others in mind, which is exactly why they collide — Vaethoran uniformity, Verathi per-room legitimacy, and Kethara built-it-so-I-run-it competence aren't competing versions of the same idea. They're different ideas about what makes an answer valid at all.

---

## Verathi — Convocation

The model itself is fully specified in `docs/world/PEOPLES.md`: no central authority, each threshold settlement holds its own Convocation, legitimacy earned through persuasion rather than counted votes or inherited office. Two things about how that actually works day to day are worth stating outright.

**Coexisting rulings.** A Convocation's ruling binds that settlement for as long as the argument that produced it holds the room. It does not bind the next settlement over. When two Convocations reach opposite conclusions on the same question — a land dispute, how to treat a Delhari expedition passing through, whether a fragment of technology is safe to keep — that isn't an error waiting to be reconciled. Verathi don't ask "which Convocation was right." They ask "was the argument made, and did it hold, here." A Vaethoran administrator reads this as inconsistency, or worse, an absence of law. A Verathi reads Vaethoran uniformity as the opposite failure: the same answer applied everywhere, regardless of whether it was ever actually argued anywhere.

**No citizenship.** There is no Verathi status to hold — no register, no birthright office, no rank carried from one settlement to the next. Standing exists only in the room, only for as long as you're winning the argument you're currently making. A Verathi elder persuasive in one Convocation may say nothing at all in the next and lose nothing by it — she simply hasn't made her case there yet. This is the literal mechanism behind "stewards, not owners": nothing is held. Everything is re-earned.

## Vaethori — Imperial Administration

Formal, hierarchical, and uniform by design. A Colonial Governor, appointed from the home empire, sits atop a provincial structure — administrative divisions like the now-lost Tertian Province — each run by an appointed magistrate answerable up the chain, not to the people they govern. Below that: census officers who record every resident whether or not the resident asked to be recorded, tribute collectors, and a standing garrison and legion system enforcing codified law uniformly, regardless of which culture actually holds the ground a given fort sits on.

**The bureaucracy/lived-identity gap.** The census doesn't ask what culture anyone belongs to. A Kethara townsperson born and raised under Vaethoran administration is, on a scroll filed in Caestum, a "Vaethoran provincial subject" — a legal category, taxed, counted, garrisoned. In every practical sense — language, values, what home means to them — they remain entirely Kethara. Vaethora doesn't experience this as a contradiction, because Vaethoran law was never built to describe culture. It was built to describe territory. The gap is invisible from inside the administration and completely obvious to anyone standing in a Kethara town paying tribute they never personally agreed to.

## Kethara — Town Councils

Informal and earned, never inherited. A Kethara council exists because decisions needed making, not because a charter said so. Authority accrues to whoever built the well, keeps the ledger straight, or has fixed the same problem three times running — a visible track record, nothing more mystical than that. Disputes get settled practically: whoever's positioned to fix the problem usually ends up deciding it, and if they get it wrong, they lose that standing next time, no formal removal required.

There's no Kethara law beyond what a given town has agreed works, which makes it entirely compatible with living under Vaethoran administration or beside Verathi land. Kethara governance is a layer of practical problem-solving running underneath whatever formal structure happens to be nominally in charge.

---

## Vethri — Near-Silent Consensus

Barely a model, because Vethri settlements are usually too small to need one. Deference runs to whoever has the clearest read on void-state risk — not to rank, age, or lineage. If a Vethri says don't camp there, the settlement doesn't camp there, and nobody thinks to formalize that into an office. Consensus is reached the way everything else Vethri do gets reached: with as few words as the situation allows, and exactly as much structure as survival actually requires — no more.

---

## Delhari — Guests Under Host Law

No native model, because Delhari haven't settled long enough to need one. They operate as guests under whatever law is already standing where they are — Kethara custom in Kethara towns, Vaethoran code in imperial territory (formalized, per `docs/world/DIPLOMACY.md`, as "the Compact"). This isn't reluctant compliance; governance simply falls outside the scope of what they came to Aevorn to do. A Delhari expedition carries its own internal hierarchy — funding, seniority, whoever holds the working theory — but that hierarchy has no standing beyond the expedition itself.

---

## Sirathi — Mutual Aid

Obligation-based, not rule-based. Sirathi settlements organize around mutual aid bonded by shared loss — nobody in a settlement that survived an Unmooring needs it explained why everyone owes everyone. Authority accrues to whoever organizes survival best: who keeps the water running, who keeps the account of who was lost and where they were from. Memory-keeping isn't ceremonial among the Sirathi — it's load-bearing civic infrastructure, on par with any practical task, because a settlement that forgets what it's rebuilding from has forgotten what it's for.
