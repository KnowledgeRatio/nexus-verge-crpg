# Nexus Verge — Diplomacy & Inter-Culture Relations

Six cultures, fifteen pairwise relationships. None of them are treaties in the way Vaethoran paperwork imagines relationships — most are unstated equilibrium, tested only when scarcity, crisis, or a particularly stubborn administrator forces the question.

---

## Vaethori Relations

### Vaethori – Verathi
Fully covered in `docs/world/PEOPLES.md` (Vaethori section) — the tensest relationship in Aevorn. Formal respect extended to Verathi elders, their actual authority quietly ignored. No open conflict. No resolution.

### Vaethori – Kethara — "Tax-bringers"
Transactional and mutually dependent. Kethara resent the tribute enough to have named the Vaethori for it — half joking, half not — but rely on Vaethoran roads and garrisons all the same; nobody tears up a bridge over how it got paid for. Vaethora, in turn, needs Kethara settlements to administer — an empire with roads and no towns along them is just an empire with roads.

### Vaethori – Vethri — "the Loud Order"
Low-grade, constant friction. Vaethoran road- and fort-building near Void-adjacent land reads, against Vethri conservation-of-everything values, as noise spent for its own sake — resources poured toward a boundary that doesn't care about roads. Vethri call them "the Loud Order" as description, not insult. There's no incident to point to. It's a standing irritant that never resolves and never escalates.

### Vaethori – Delhari — "the Compact"
Regulated tolerance, formalized by Delhari themselves as "the Compact": operate under permit and license in imperial territory, and Vaethora looks past most of what the permit doesn't cover. Good trade both ways — cheap intelligence for the empire, borrowed legitimacy for Delhari, who otherwise hold no standing anywhere in Aevorn.

### Vaethori – Sirathi
The empire's "protection and belonging" pitch gets tested hardest here. Formal resettlement, and something close to citizenship, is offered to Sirathi survivors in exchange for loyalty and legion labor — and depending entirely on the administrator making the offer, it lands as genuine belonging or as an empire recruiting desperate people into its garrisons at a discount. Vaethora believes its own pitch here more sincerely than almost anywhere else. That doesn't guarantee it lands the same way for whoever's receiving it.

## Kethara Relations

### Kethara – Verathi
Old-timer respect, no political friction underneath it. Kethara towns treat resident Verathi the way you'd treat someone who was there before the town existed — deferred to, asked for context, never obeyed. Verathi have no more interest in running Kethara towns than in running anything else; they've outlived generations of them without once trying to.

### Kethara – Delhari
Low-friction host and guest. Delhari money and knowledge are genuinely welcome — they spend, they trade information, they occasionally fix something nobody local could. Their condescension is a mild, ongoing tax on the relationship, not a breaking one; a Kethara innkeeper has heard "the entropic boundary event" often enough to just nod and keep pouring.

### Kethara – Sirathi
Unexpectedly the easiest fit in the whole chart. Kethara's flat, unsentimental "so what are you going to do about it" lands better on Sirathi grief than sympathy ever could — it doesn't ask anyone to perform loss or hurry past it, just to keep building. Sirathi who settle in Kethara towns tend to stay.

### Kethara – Vethri
Rare contact, real respect. Kethara don't see Vethri often, and find them genuinely unsettling when they do, but trust a Vethri's read on void-risk over almost anyone's — including their own instincts. "If a Vethri tells you not to camp there, you don't camp there" functions as closer to Kethara civic law than anything actually voted on.

## Vethri Relations

### Vethri – Sirathi
The closest bond of any pair in Aevorn. Both share an unsentimental relationship to loss — neither performs grief nor asks the other to — and void-adjacent settlements already skew toward both together (see `docs/world/PEOPLES.md`, "Writing Across Cultures"). Where you find one, you often find the other, and it works precisely because neither asks the other to be anything they're not.

### Vethri – Delhari
Mutual mild irritation, low stakes. Delhari researchers want to study Vethri as a subject — the only culture with real anthropological knowledge of void-state behavior is, naturally, an irresistible research target. Vethri's entire discipline is editing everything down to what's load-bearing, which leaves a Delhari theorist with almost nothing to work with and no polite way to ask for more.

### Vethri – Verathi
Mutual regard between the two oldest, most calibrated cultures in Aevorn. Their methods are opposites — Verathi debate everything, Vethri say almost nothing — but the seriousness underneath is identical, and each recognizes it in the other without needing it explained. Neither has to perform legitimacy for the other; that's rare for either of them.

## Verathi Relations

### Verathi – Delhari
Low-temperature disdain, mostly amused rather than threatened. Verathi have watched scholars arrive with frameworks built somewhere else and confidently misapplied for longer than most Delhari origin-worlds have existed. It isn't hostility — it's the specific patience of someone who has seen this exact conversation before and already knows how it ends.

### Verathi – Sirathi
Genuinely sympathetic, and it fits naturally. Archiving fragmented memory against loss is the Verathi's whole civic function — it's what they already do for the dimension itself — so extending that same care to what individual Sirathi lost isn't a stretch of their role. It's an application of it.

---

## Delhari – Sirathi
The most uncomfortable pairing in the chart. Delhari theorists treat Unmooring survivor accounts as research data — valuable, rare, first-hand — and that framing can read, correctly, as mining someone's trauma for a paper nobody in Aevorn asked for. Sirathi patience runs deep. This is where it runs out fastest.

## When Standing Friction Doesn't Hold

None of the above is fixed. Where apex-riftborn dominance or contested-resource pressure runs high — a border where a Dominant-tier predator has the stronger claim than anyone's garrison, a riftborn seam or tech relic nobody can afford to lose to the Void or to a rival claim (see `docs/world/CREATURES.md`, "Contested Ground") — standing cultural friction gets set aside, temporarily and without ceremony. A Vaethoran garrison and a Kethara town that resents its tribute will still share a wall against something that doesn't recognize either of their claims. The friction returns once the immediate pressure passes. It was never resolved — only outweighed for as long as the shared threat was bigger than the argument.
