# Nexus Verge — Peoples & Cultures

Six cultures exist in Aevorn. They are not races — each is multi-racial. They are defined by *when* someone arrived and *why they stayed*.

Collectively, Aevorn's playable and humanoid peoples are called **the Wrought** — those who shape, as against the Voidborn (unmade, unraveling) and riftborn fauna (shaped by their environment, not by will). It's a descriptive term, not a political one, and crosses every culture line below. See `docs/world/CREATURES.md` for how it sits against everything else that lives here.

---

## Verathi

**Who they are:** The oldest inhabitants. They didn't arrive — they *are* the dimension. Multi-racial through millennia of convergence events. Archivists of fragmented memory, and — more than that — philosopher-citizens who govern by open debate. They live where biomes meet, believing edges are where truth is sharpest.

There is no Verathi nation and no central Verathi authority. Each threshold settlement holds its own **Convocation** — an open assembly where legitimacy is earned through persuasion, not counted in votes or inherited through office. A settlement's Convocation can, and often does, reach a different conclusion from the Convocation three days' walk away, and neither considers the other wrong — only differently argued. This fragmentation isn't a flaw they're working to fix. It's the shape their politics has always had: poleis answering to nobody but their own room.

Their civic doctrine is explicit, not just a temperament: **stewards, not owners.** No Verathi holds land, precedent, or interpretation as a final possession — only as a position to be defended in the next debate. What looks, to a Vaethoran administrator, like an absence of government is in fact a government that never stopped being argued.

**Exonyms:** "Old Blood," "the Rooted"

**Names:**
- First: Aerindel, Carveth, Osílen, Marath, Silven, Cael, Erevorn, Caelindra
- Places: Aethenmoor, Ondavel, Caelindra, Silvareth, Erevorn
- *Pattern: flowing, compound, meaning-heavy. Soft consonants, open vowels.*

**Voice in dialogue:** Measured and allusive. They speak in layers — a statement that is also a question. They rarely answer directly. They reference what has been rather than what is. A Verathi merchant doesn't say "that item is rare." They say "I haven't seen one since the Ashfield convergence. That was before you were born." They are not evasive — they are simply speaking from a much longer timeline than you are. This is also the register of the Convocation floor: a Verathi arguing policy sounds the same as a Verathi remembering the Ashfield convergence, because to them persuasion and memory are the same discipline.

## Kethara

**Who they are:** 2–5 generations settled. Descendants of early arrivals who stopped being *from* somewhere else. They built the towns. They speak a pragmatic pidgin that swallowed most other languages. Wildly multiracial. Culture of pragmatic solidarity — zero sentimentality for lost homeworlds. What matters is what you build here.

**Exonyms:** "Landers," "the Settled"

**Names:**
- First: Dren, Sova, Torric, Kael, Rythe, Ash, Marro
- Places: Karath Crossing, Dyreth Hold, Sova's Rest, Ember Post
- *Pattern: short, clipped, mixed phonetics. Functional.*

**Voice in dialogue:** Direct and pragmatic. No ceremony. They say what they mean and expect you to do the same. Sentimentality makes them uncomfortable. They acknowledge the Void with the same tone they use for bad weather — not minimising, just refusing to catastrophise. "It's coming. So what are you going to do about it?" A Kethara NPC who is warm is warm because they *chose* to be, not because they're deferential.

## Vethri

**Who they are:** Settled at or near the Void boundary — by choice or by drift. Not corrupted. *Calibrated.* Culture of silence, minimalism, and absolute conservation of energy and expectation. They waste nothing. They believe nothing is permanent. The only culture with real anthropological knowledge of void-state behaviour. Most of the Verge thinks they are touched. They may be the only people who understand what is actually coming.

**Exonyms:** "The Silent," "Null-touched" *(slur — do not use in non-hostile NPC dialogue)*

**Names:**
- First: Vec, Ren, Os, Sil, Nam, Vel, Nun
- Places: Null, The Between, Thin, The Shelf, Near
- *Pattern: monosyllabic or near-monosyllabic. Worn down to minimum.*

**Voice in dialogue:** Minimal and precise. Every word is load-bearing — they have edited out everything else. They do not greet. They do not farewell. They answer the question you asked, not the question underneath it. A Vethri giving directions: "North. Three days. Don't camp near the rift." If they volunteer information you didn't ask for, pay attention — it means they think you need it and won't figure it out yourself.

## Delhari

**Who they are:** Active arrivals — here deliberately, in the last generation, for something specific. Scholars, merchants, power-seekers, dimensional theorists, cults convinced the nexus holds cosmic answers. They have not settled. They use Kethara infrastructure while pursuing their objective. When they find it — or don't — most leave. Some stay and become Kethara.

**Exonyms:** "Seekers," "fresh boots," "the Arrivals"

**Names:**
- First: Thaddeus, Lyris, Orvek, Cassia, Maren, Aldric, Voss
- Places: None of their own — they use existing settlements
- *Pattern: more formal, varied in origin, often academic or merchant-class in flavour.*

**Voice in dialogue:** Precise but often condescending — they arrived knowing what they came for, and they haven't fully recalibrated to the reality of the place. They over-explain. They use terminology from wherever they came from that doesn't quite map. A Delhari scholar doesn't say "the Void" — they say "the entropic boundary event." They are not stupid; they are operating with a framework that was built somewhere else and hasn't cracked yet.

---

## Sirathi

**Who they are:** Void refugees. Their home regions — whether in Nexus Verge or in dimensions that fed into it — have been consumed or are going. They carry the specific grief of people who watched reality end. Their settlements are named after what they lost. Considered unlucky by some. Treated with suspicion by others. The most motivated people on the Verge — they have seen what happens when motivation runs out.

**Exonyms:** "The Unmoored," "void-fled"

**Names:**
- First: Kel, Tyn, Moru, Asha, Var, Seren, Onn
- Places: "Old Moru," "First Town," "The Before" *(always ironic or aching)*
- *Pattern: short, cut off — like their history.*

**Voice in dialogue:** Weight in everything they say. Not melodramatic — the opposite. They have already done the grief and come through it. What remains is purpose and a very dry awareness that the things people worry about here are minor. They are not trying to be dark. They are simply calibrated to a different scale of loss. A Sirathi NPC at a tavern, hearing someone complain about the weather: a long pause, then "Yes. It's very cold."

## Writing Across Cultures

When a player talks to an NPC, the culture should be audible before the name is stated.

- **Mixed settlement:** Kethara make up most of any town. One or two Verathi who've been there longer than the town has. A handful of Delhari passing through. One Sirathi who hasn't left.
- **Void-adjacent settlement:** Majority Vethri or Sirathi. Other cultures more unsettled, quieter.
- **Ruins and frontier:** Verathi and Vethri who know the land. Everyone else is new.

**What the name "Nexus Verge" signals in dialogue:** Anyone who says "Nexus Verge" in casual conversation is either Delhari (just arrived), or very new to anywhere. Old Blood say "Aevorn" or nothing. Kethara say "the Verge." Vethri say "here."

## Vaethori

**Who they are:** Citizens of Vaethora — a vast inter-dimensional empire that colonized Nexus Verge roughly four generations ago through deliberate expedition, not migration. They built the roads between the towns Kethara built. They keep the census. They maintain the forts. They drafted the trade law that most settlements operate under, whether they know it or not. Their pitch is genuine: *Pax Vaethora* — law, infrastructure, protection, belonging to something larger. The military exists to hold the peace, not to make examples. But they are absolutely a colonial power: they collect tribute, appoint governors, and their opinions about land ownership are backed by forts.

The home empire still exists. A Colonial Governor is appointed from Vaethora. The relationship to the home empire is strained by distance and the difficulty of communicating across dimensions — but it has not broken.

**Exonyms:** "The Builders," "Road-makers," "the Compact" *(Delhari — neutral/respectful)*, "Tax-bringers" *(Kethara — half joking, half not)*, "the Loud Order" *(Vethri — their infrastructure activity near Void-adjacent land is a constant irritant)*

**Relationship to Verathi:** The tensest. The Verathi were here by eons. They have no interest in Vaethoran law and considerable contempt for it. The Vaethori extend formal respect to Verathi elders while quietly ignoring their actual authority. No open conflict. No resolution.

Few Vaethori administrators know, or care to advertise, that Vaethoran Stoicism itself descends from centuries of Verathi ethical debate — the same Convocation tradition, systemized generations later into doctrine and drilled into soldiers as settled procedure. It is, from the Verathi side, exactly the kind of collapse they hold in contempt: a living argument frozen into an answer.

**The Void:** A culture built on permanence — roads, records, legacy, the long arc of civilizational progress — confronting something that erases permanence absolutely. Their Stoic philosophy (*concern yourself with what is in your capacity; accept what is not; do your duty*) is both a genuine resource and, in the face of the Void, a potentially tragic one. They will build fortifications at the boundary. They will keep records of every settlement lost. They will hold lines long past when holding makes strategic sense, because admitting the empire cannot protect what it claimed is not something the administrative mind easily does.

**Names:**
- First (male): Maeren, Valus, Caston, Dorian, Luvar, Servian, Corvin, Malius, Aethan, Caerus, Toran, Quinthar
- First (female): Vaelia, Cassara, Maeris, Doriva, Servana, Corvela, Tasia, Liova, Caelia, Aethis
- Family names *(used formally — "Maeren of House Caetor," "Servana Dorvath")*: Vaelen, Caetor, Maethos, Dorvath, Servani, Corvath
- Places: Caestum, Nova Vaethora, Dorium, Aethaneum, Fort Corvel, Maeris Station
- *Pattern: Latin phonemes — open vowels, hard consonants, -us/-a endings, three-syllable given names common. More formal than Kethara, less compound than Verathi. Place suffixes: -um, -ium, -eum. Military installations: "Fort [name]."*

**Voice in dialogue:** Formal but not cold — they believe in what they represent. A Vaethori administrator explains the law in full, expects compliance, and is genuinely puzzled when this isn't received as a gift. Full names on introduction. Conversations concluded, not ended. When moved, they understate it — emotion is present but contained within the structure of duty. A Vaethori soldier at the Void boundary, watching another settlement go: *"Tertian Province is now considered non-viable. The records have been committed to the archive at Caestum. The garrison has been reassigned."* Said with no drama. Said with everything suppressed beneath procedure, because procedure is what holds when everything else doesn't.

## Implementation: Procedural Naming

All six cultures are wired into procedural NPC generation as of 2026-06-16. Mechanical race (`data/races.json` — human, elf, dwarf, halfling, dragonborn) and culture (`data/cultures.json`) are separate axes, matching "not races — each is multi-racial": `NPCGenerator` rolls a culture by weighted `npcWeight` to pick the name pool, independent of any race roll. Race-based naming only applies as a fallback for campaigns with no culture data (e.g. `core`).

Name pools live in `data/npcNames.json` as `{culture}FirstNames` / `{culture}PlaceNames`, all `campaignIds: ["nexus-verge"]`. Vaethori is the only culture with a dedicated `vaethoriLastNames` pool (its formal family names); the other five fall back to the shared generic `lastNames` pool, matching how core races already work.

Settlement-level culture weighting (void-adjacent settlements skewing Vethri/Sirathi, per "Writing Across Cultures" above) is not yet implemented — settlement objects carry no terrain/region data to key off of. Current weights are a flat baseline for any settlement.
