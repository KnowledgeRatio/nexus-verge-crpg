## Core Identity

Nexus Verge (Verathi name: **Aevorn**, "the deep weaving") is a convergence dimension — a plane at the intersection of countless others. Over uncounted ages it has pulled matter, ecology, and peoples into itself. Every biome exists because some fragment of another world fell in and took root.

It is known. People arrive deliberately. It has the energy of a frontier in full swing: competing interests, improvised law, towns that exist because someone planted a flag. And at the edges of the world, something is eating the light.

**Tone:** Desperate wonder. Survival on a living frontier.

## What Draws People Here

Three things, in descending order of how openly people admit to them.

### 1. Riftborn Materials
Matter that has passed through dimensional membranes over millennia and acquired composite properties that cannot exist in single-dimension worlds. The best tools, weapons, and components anywhere are made from riftborn materials. They only form at convergence points. They cannot be manufactured elsewhere.

### 2. Wayfinding
Nexus Verge is the only stable platform in the known multiverse for studying dimensional navigation from the inside. The ability to move between dimensions reliably is the ultimate power in a multiverse. The Verathi have been developing this knowledge for eons. Others come to learn it, buy it, or take it.

### 3. The Boundary
Where void-state meets matter-space, something is produced. The Vethri call it **null-flux**. Some believe it is the most energetically potent substance in existence. Everyone who has tried to harvest it in quantity has been consumed. This does not stop people from trying.

## Economy

Material scarcity is theoretically defeatable here — infinite dimensional access means any material resource can be sourced. What becomes valuable instead:

- **Reputation** — who trusts you, who will deal with you
- **Knowledge** — what only you understand
- **Access** — who you know and where you can go
- **Riftborn materials** — the only things that genuinely cannot be replicated

This is why the game's reputation economy is the correct economy for this world, not gold.

## The Void

The Void is a neighboring dimension — a true abyss of thermodynamic completion. Pure entropy. Absolute silence. It exists adjacent to Nexus Verge and its boundary is slowly, unevenly, advancing.

The Void does not approach like an army. It arrives. First birds stop. Then insects. Then wind. Then a region becomes absent — not dark, not dead. Just *not there*.

### The Voidborn
Entities originating from the Void. Non-living (never alive — not undead) or non-corporeal. They are not organisms in any biological sense. They are concentrations of void-state that propagate toward complexity. Not intelligence. Not hunger. Physics. The void-state advances fastest in the presence of ordered matter — life, thought, structure.

They don't know you exist. That is what makes them unsurvivable.

**Practically:**
- You cannot kill them. You disrupt a concentration, scatter it, seal the rift it is propagating through.
- What the game presents as "monsters" are void-state concentrations — phenomena, not creatures with feelings or intent.
- Major concentrations at rift points function as the game's bosses: things to be disrupted or sealed, not defeated in the traditional sense.

### Going to the Void
The Void exists as a place. Whether the player travels there is a narrative question and possible endgame territory. It is not required by the world design.

## The Hero

Arrives from outside Nexus Verge or from within it — **player-defined**. Origin is open. No assumed cultural background is forced on the player. The **Calling** carries the identity work.

The Callings are not guilds, classes, or formal institutions. Nobody in Aevorn walks around calling themselves a Scholar. The Callings describe what kind of person faces the end of the world in a particular way — and that is a question the player answers for themselves:

- **Dedication** — faces it by committing to something living. A person, a place, an ideal. Martial strength as an expression of will.
- **Scholar** — faces it by needing to understand it first. To know is to survive.
- **Wanderlust** — faces it by refusing to be caught still. Freedom and cunning as a philosophy.

Specialisations are refinements of this identity — a more precise form of the core calling, not a branch of a class tree. They emerge from experience, not selection menus.

## The Five Cultures

### Verathi
The oldest inhabitants. Multi-racial through millennia of convergence events — they didn't arrive, they *are* the dimension. Archivists of fragmented memory. They live where biomes meet, believing edges are where truth is sharpest. Consider themselves stewards of Aevorn, not owners of it.

Exonyms: "Old Blood," "the Rooted"

**Character names:** Aerindel, Carveth, Osílen, Marath, Silven, Cael
**Place names:** Aethenmoor, Ondavel, Caelindra, Silvareth, Erevorn

*Pattern: flowing, compound, meaning-heavy. Soft consonants, open vowels.*

---

### Kethara
2–5 generations settled. Descendants of early arrivals who stopped being *from* somewhere else. They built the towns. They speak a pragmatic pidgin that swallowed most other languages. Wildly multiracial. Culture of pragmatic solidarity — zero sentimentality for lost homeworlds. What matters is what you build here.

Exonyms: "Landers," "the Settled"

**Character names:** Dren, Sova, Torric, Kael, Rythe, Ash, Marro
**Place names:** Karath Crossing, Dyreth Hold, Sova's Rest, Ember Post

*Pattern: short, clipped, mixed phonetics. Functional.*

---

### Vethri
Settled at or near the Void boundary — by choice or by drift. Not corrupted. *Calibrated.* Culture of silence, minimalism, and absolute conservation of energy and expectation. They waste nothing. They believe nothing is permanent. The only culture with real anthropological knowledge of void-state behavior. Most of the Verge thinks they are touched. They may be the only people who understand what is coming.

Exonyms: "The Silent," "Null-touched" *(slur)*

**Character names:** Vec, Ren, Os, Sil, Nam, Vel, Nun
**Place names:** Null, The Between, Thin, The Shelf, Near

*Pattern: monosyllabic or near-monosyllabic. Worn down to minimum.*

---

### Delhari
Active arrivals — here deliberately, in the last generation, for something specific. Scholars, merchants, power-seekers, dimensional theorists, cults convinced the nexus holds cosmic answers. They have not settled. They use Kethara infrastructure while pursuing their objective. When they find it — or don't — most leave. Some stay.

Exonyms: "Seekers," "fresh boots," "the Arrivals"

**Character names:** Thaddeus, Lyris, Orvek, Cassia, Maren, Aldric, Voss
**Place names:** None of their own — they use existing settlements.

*Pattern: more formal, varied in origin, often academic in flavor.*

---

### Sirathi
Void refugees. Their home regions — whether in Nexus Verge or in dimensions that fed into it — have been consumed or are going. They carry the specific grief of people who watched reality end. Their settlements are named after what they lost. Considered unlucky by some. Treated with suspicion by others. The most motivated people on the Verge.

Exonyms: "The Unmoored," "void-fled"

**Character names:** Kel, Tyn, Moru, Asha, Var, Seren, Onn
**Place names:** "Old Moru," "First Town," "The Before" *(always ironic or aching)*

*Pattern: short, cut off — like their history.*

## Content Implementation Notes

When writing any player-visible text, the following apply:

- **"Nexus Verge"** is the formal / outsider label. NPCs from older cultures say "the Verge" or nothing at all.
- **Voidborn** are never described with emotional language — no rage, no hunger, no malice. They are weather. They are physics.
- **Items** with riftborn material components should hint at their impossible composite nature — properties that shouldn't coexist.
- **NPC voice** varies by culture: Verathi are measured and allusive; Kethara are direct and pragmatic; Vethri are minimal and precise; Delhari are precise but often condescending; Sirathi carry weight in everything they say.
- **Economy**: NPCs do not prize gold. They prize reputation, favors, information, and access. Gold exists but is not the first language of value.
