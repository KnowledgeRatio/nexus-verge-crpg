# Nexus Verge — Image Generation Style Guide

This file is injected verbatim into AI image prompts. Each section is used for its matching asset type. Keep instructions direct and unambiguous — this is read by an image model, not a human.

---

## Art Style

Oil painting style for all monster, item, and portrait assets. Visible brushwork, textured impasto surface, paint-material quality throughout. Desaturated earth tones — ash grey, iron brown, deep slate — with isolated warm pops of amber or ember light. Mood: weathered and strange, a living frontier where multiple worlds have collided and taken root. No photo-realism. No anime. No bright saturated fantasy. No white border, no frame, no bezel, no vignette border. Every asset carries evidence of the world's convergence: mismatched materials, two-climate details, or rift-geometry seams — no object or creature is wholly from one world. Equipment and surfaces show use — scars, repairs, and improvisation — but do not exaggerate decay. Worn, not ruined. Lighting: single warm source from upper-left, cool fill from opposite side. Apply to all asset types.

## World Setting

Nexus Verge (called "the Verge" in common speech) is a convergence dimension — a plane that has been pulling matter, ecology, and peoples from other dimensions for ages. It is a living frontier with competing factions and improvised law. At the world's edges the Void advances: pure entropy, no intent, no hunger, just the slow erasure of ordered matter. Tone: desperate wonder, survival on a frontier that is still being mapped. All content is medieval-fantasy in material culture. No industrial equipment, no modern technology, no post-apocalyptic ruin.

## Terrain Tiles

Top-down orthographic view, hand-painted cartographic map tile style — like an old illustrated atlas rendered in oil paint. Earthy and organic. Textures suggest layered origin: each terrain is a fragment of another world that took root, with edges that don't quite match their neighbours and flora that belongs to two climates at once. Each biome uses one identifying accent: marshes use sickly yellow-green, forests use dark teal shadow, mountains use blue-grey frost, plains use pale straw. No characters or creatures. Seamless edges. Absolutely no grid lines, no graph paper, no graph texture, no ruled lines, no grid overlay, no squared paper look. At biome boundaries, render a visible convergence seam — a thin line where two terrain types meet with an abrupt material shift, not a gradient blend.

## Monster Art

Oil painting. Three-quarter view, creature fills 70% of frame. Dramatic but grounded pose — threat-display, not heroic. Background: ancient stone, dense atmospheric fog, or blurred dimensional-rift environment — dark and unspecific, no competing detail. Medieval-fantasy setting only: no modern technology, no industrial elements, no post-apocalyptic debris, no electric lighting, no mechanical parts. Beasts and humanoid enemies from other dimensions are naturalistic but wrong — wrong proportions, wrong climate, visibly out of place. Lighting from upper-left, single warm source. No white border, no frame, no bezel. Non-voidborn creatures from other dimensions should appear climatically or proportionally wrong — too dense for their size, fur adapted to a different climate, eyes built for different light. Keep the wrongness biological and grounded, not exaggerated.

## Item Icons

Oil painting. Single item centred on near-black background. No hands, no environment, no border, no frame, no bezel, no vignette border. Materials rendered with clear texture: leather shows cracked grain, iron shows hammer marks, wood shows split grain. Magic items carry a restrained inner glow — unsettling, not triumphant. Rarity by colour temperature: common = raw iron and worn leather; uncommon = faint blue-silver sheen; rare = cold violet edge-light; legendary = deep amber aura with visible heat distortion. Lighting from upper-left. Items made from riftborn materials show two incompatible material properties coexisting in the same surface — a blade that has metal grain on one face and dense banding on another, a stone component that fractures differently on each half, a resin element with inclusions that drift against gravity. Render the material impossibility as a precise physical fact, not as magic glow or distortion. The wrongness should be observable, not atmospheric.

## Character Portraits

Oil painting bust portrait. Subject centred, face occupying top half of frame. Expression: serious and watchful — not smiling. Background soft-blurred and culture-appropriate: Kethara subjects in pragmatic stone-and-timber settlements; Verathi subjects at biome edges where two ecologies meet; Vethri subjects in minimal open terrain near a void boundary; Delhari subjects in structured interiors or transit camps; Sirathi subjects in sparse outposts that look newly built and already grieving. Costume is functional and worn — no ornate decoration unless the character is explicitly nobility. No white border, no frame, no bezel.
