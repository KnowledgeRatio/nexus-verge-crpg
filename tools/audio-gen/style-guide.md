# Nexus Verge audio direction

Use small, worn acoustic and found-material sounds to convey inhabited places at the edge of a dangerous, living world. Keep space for player decisions, readable combat effects, and quiet moments. The same restrained three-note motif can link exploration and combat.

Music is instrumental. Combat music supplies a continuous musical bed; it contains no weapon, impact, movement, or other action sounds. Those effects play separately when the game action occurs. Leave room for them through arrangement and level, without prompting musical gaps or breaks. Avoid American Western signifiers (twang guitar, harmonica, saloon piano, galloping rhythms), heroic orchestra, choir, trailer impacts, and dance drums. Generated music is a candidate for an edited loop; audition the seam in game.

Long combat cues develop a recognizable motif through changing register, counterline, voicing, and harmony. Keep the low-string and hand-drum drive while the melody evolves; a short four-bar pattern repeated unchanged should not carry the whole fight. Join related passages into a longer arc before looping.

Effects are short, isolated, and physically legible. An action's release must not reveal whether it hits; contact and healing play when the visible outcome occurs. Avoid speech, music tails, exaggerated bass, and gore. Generate reusable families, not one effect per item or ability.

Keep source prompts and settings with every candidate. The generated output is not assumed exclusive or reproducible from a prompt.
